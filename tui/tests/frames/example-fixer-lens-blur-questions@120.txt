Lens Blur wireframes (BLR-20)  updated 10m ago
The Lens Blur panel in Fixer's Develop and its tools on the photo, drawn from the design document of 14 October 2026 and
the BLR-20 note. The feature was parked by the owner on 14 October 2026, since it needs more research and calibration:
its rows, BLR-20 and BLR-21 to BLR-28, are Deferred, with their phase set to Later. These wireframes record the design
for when it resumes, so every Lens Blur region is Later and the questions stay open; the refinement brushes (BLR-25)
will also wait on counsel's patent search (DSN-31). The parts of the app drawn around them, the Develop window, its
other panels, the tool strip and the photo, are Built.

 1 Where does the Lens Blur panel sit among Develop's panels?
   The design doesn't place it, and the BLR-20 note doesn't record where Lightroom puts its own. Develop's nine panels
   follow Lightroom's order (PanelID): Basic, Tone Curve, Color Mixer, Color Grading, Detail, Lens Corrections,
   Transform, Effects, Calibration. The blur renders after Retouch and before Detail, and Effects already holds eight
   groups in 26 rows, Grain among them.
   A. A panel of its own after Effects, before Calibration, with the panels that change a photo's character; nothing
   above it moves.
   B. A group inside Effects, beside Grain and Halation: no tenth panel, but Apply, the depth line and the canvas tools
   in the middle of a long panel, and Effects' eye turning both off.
   C. A panel of its own before Detail, in the order it renders: the blur first, then sharpening and noise reduction on
   the blurred photo.
   My lean: A: it's an effect, and a panel of its own keeps its eye, its Edited chip and Solo Mode, which a group inside
   Effects would share.

 2 How is focus set on the photo?
   The design focuses on the depth under a click, or on the range under a dragged rectangle. In Develop a click on the
   photo is Lightroom's click-to-zoom, between fit and 100%. Tools that take a click on the photo, White Balance's
   eyedropper (W) and Point Color's, are turned on by a button in their panel, and a pill over the photo says what a
   click does and that Esc cancels.
   A. A Point focus button beside Focus on, as the eyedropper is: while it's on, a click focuses on a point and a drag
   on an area, until Esc or the button again.
   B. A click or a drag on the photo focuses whenever the Lens Blur panel is expanded, and click-to-zoom waits until
   it's collapsed.
   C. Both: the button, and a click on the photo while the panel is expanded.
   My lean: A: a panel can stay expanded for a whole session, so B and C would take click-to-zoom away for as long, and
   a button with its pill is how Develop already takes clicks on the photo.

 3 How is the Focus Range drawn?
   The design has two ends over a histogram of the photo's depth, on the Depth Range mask's scale (0 far, 100 near), the
   blur growing from each end as a lens's does. The Depth Range mask's bar has four handles (where the range starts, is
   full from, stops being full and ends) over a dark-to-light bar, Far on the left and Near on the right, a value field
   for each handle and no histogram. Lightroom writes four numbers for its range and shows a box with two ends.
   A. Two ends over the histogram, as Lightroom's box: drag a side to move one end, or the box to move both.
   B. The Depth Range mask's four handles, reused: the inner two bound the focus and the outer two set where the blur
   reaches full strength, a blur that stops growing, unlike a lens's, and two more numbers in the edit.
   C. A, labelled as the Depth Range bar is: Far on the left, Near on the right and a value field for each end, so the
   two controls read as one scale (as drawn).
   My lean: C: a lens has one range in focus and a blur that grows from its ends, so two ends are the whole model, and
   the mask's labels and fields make it plain the two share a scale.

 4 How are the shapes chosen?
   The design has Lightroom's five shapes and adds Blades (5 to 9) and Rotation for the bladed one. A panel's rows are
   280 points wide, 198 beside a label: five icons fit there and five names don't, which is why Upright's six buttons
   sit in two rows of three. Elsewhere in Develop a choice is a pop-up menu (Treatment, the mask brush, Healing's Mode)
   or, in Color Grading, a segmented control of icons.
   A. Five icons in a segmented control, as Color Grading's view and Lightroom's buttons, each named in its tooltip;
   Blades and Rotation appear under it while 5-Blade is chosen.
   B. A pop-up menu of the five by name, as Treatment and Healing's Mode; Blades and Rotation appear under it while
   5-Blade is chosen.
   C. A's icons, with Blades and Rotation always there and dimmed unless 5-Blade is chosen, so the rows below stay put
   while shapes are tried.
   My lean: C: shapes are told apart by eye and tried one after another, so icons that change in one click suit them,
   and fixed rows keep Cat Eye and Boost from moving each time.

 5 Where does Grain start?
   The design puts the photo's own noise back into what the blur smoothed, from its noise model and in proportion to how
   much each pixel was blurred: Grain, 0 to 100, defaults to 100, the photo's own level. Lightroom has no such control
   and leaves users to add Grain through a mask. Effects already has a Grain group, a film grain added on top, under the
   same name.
   A. At 100: the photo's own noise, as the design has it.
   B. Off, as Lightroom leaves it, the slider there for whoever wants the noise back.
   C. Always the photo's own noise and no slider, as Generative Remove's fills have it put back today.
   My lean: A: a blurred area without the photo's noise looks pasted on, and the slider lets a smoother look be chosen;
   the name is worth a second look beside Effects' Grain.

 6 Apply, or the panel's eye?
   The design has Lightroom's Apply checkbox, which also starts the depth's estimate the first time. Every Develop panel
   but Basic has an eye in its header that turns it off and keeps its settings (UI-12), so this panel would have two
   switches for one thing, as drawn.
   A. Apply only until the blur is first applied, as a button that estimates the depth; from then on the eye turns it
   off and on, as in every other panel, and Lightroom's Active imports to the eye.
   B. Lightroom's Apply checkbox, and no eye on this panel, as Basic has none.
   C. Both, kept as one switch: ticking Apply opens the eye, and closing the eye clears Apply.
   My lean: A: Develop keeps one way to turn a panel off, and Apply keeps the one job the eye can't do, asking for the
   depth.
